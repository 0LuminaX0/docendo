# Docendo

Learning-by-teaching study for CS-411 Digital Education (EPFL). Team: Andrii Negrub, Phil Gustke, Rafael Cruz. A learner watches a short lesson on multi-armed bandits, teaches it to an AI classmate ("Kai", placeholder name) until Kai feels ready, practises, then takes a final test. Next.js app on Vercel: https://docendo-jade.vercel.app.

Design history: https://claude.ai/artifact/TFPVnezekaraxiz7VQtxoP. Status and next steps: [PLAN.md](PLAN.md). Phil's handoff triage: https://claude.ai/artifact/CobG3jpsLRdSPhBnzumNej.

## The study

A conceptual replication of Okita & Schwartz (2013, JLS, doi 10.1080/10508406.2013.807263) Experiment 2, *recursive vs direct feedback*, with an LLM teachable agent. Both conditions watch the same lesson and teach Kai the same way; only the practice chapter differs:
- **Direct feedback (built):** the learner solves the problems, a first try and one retry each, with a right/wrong light.
- **Recursive feedback (built, `components/KaiSolve.tsx`, `KaiNotebook.tsx`, `engine/tutor/solve.ts`):** Kai can only be right when its notebook holds every fact the problem `needs` (practice.json), explained correctly (partial isn't enough), with no wrong belief left that contradicts one of those facts (an older notebook's belief, saved without its fact, still blocks its whole idea): the **gate**, enforced in code because a model told to "use only your notes" still reads rules out of the options or uses what it knows. For a fact Kai was told nothing or only vaguely about, Kai says it is guessing and reasons from its own assumption for that fact (the fact's `assume` in the graph: one known misconception per teachable fact, copied word for word into the problem's `beliefs` with the option it leads to; checked by `pnpm content validate`), so its wrong answer follows from its own steps and has a named cause: never a right line of reasoning with a wrong answer swapped in at the end (the code checks the answer matches the guess and rejects turnarounds). Kai says a guess as a beginner's hunch, not a rule (2026-11-03: the formal assumption read as calculated and harsh to someone who taught little): each assumption has a `hunch` (and `hunchVague`) in the graph, a short unsure line in plain words ("I'd guess greedy just goes with whichever looks best after the first tries and stays with it."), which the solver is given as the way to say it and the offline attempt writes after "My notes don't cover this part."; Kai never blames the learner ("you never told me"); with a notebook that meets none of the problem's needs Kai keeps it to 2 or 3 short steps (otherwise 2 to 5). `assume` stays the exact belief the practice items and the analysis refer to; `pnpm content validate` warns when an assumption has no hunch. With several facts missing, the first in `needs` order (the order the steps use them) decides, so the same notebook always fails at the same step (2026-11-02; before, a beginner's rule picked at random). A vague explanation counts as missing, unless the problem lists a vague reading (`assumeVague`, `when: vague`) that still leads to the key ("the bonus grows" without "ever more slowly" is enough to see that it grows, not that the gaps between picks grow). A misconception is what Kai was told: the model reasons from it freely. A right answer without the facts, a wrong one with them (the gate open: the model's slip, not the learner's teaching, 2026-11-05: p4's "B has the higher average and the bigger bonus" read as undecided in about 1 of 4 tries), or a try that breaks the rules gets up to two stricter retries while there's time (15 and 20 s after the first), then the offline attempt (also built on the guess; in Kai's voice: "My notes say “…”, so I'll go by that here", "So my best guess is B: …"). The judge reads a correction knowing the problem and Kai's attempt (the practice route passes both). Teaching the fact in the correction opens the gate for the retry. Options are numbered for the model (trucks and headlines are named by letters) and shown as letters to the learner. 2 to 5 steps (2 or 3 when the notebook has nothing for the problem); the model decides where a step ends (no cutting; a reply that hits the token limit is retried). Kai solves the same problems step by step with the LLM from its numbered notes only (the learner's words and still-believed misconceptions; never facts, keys or solutions; `notesOf` merges overlapping quotes of one message into the longest), each step citing the notes it used (a try before a correction shows where its notes sit in the notebook now, or a struck mark for a note the correction crossed out). Kai starts as soon as the problem is shown. Its steps are written out at reading speed (35 characters a second, 1.2 to 4 s a step), and before the light the learner calls it ("I think it's right / wrong", `practice_kai_call`): the counterpart of the direct condition, where the learner commits to an answer and then sees the light. The panel scrolls into view when it appears and, after the call, turns into the verdict (green "You called it", amber "Not this time"). Both conditions get the same feedback; nothing shows the outcome before the call (no light, face, end note, progress mark or correction box). The call is also a measure of how well learners spot Kai's errors. The page has two columns: the task (the problem, then Kai's steps with the light) and Kai's notebook beside it, sticky and as tall as the screen allows (ruled paper, the learner's words in Kai's hand, the lines a step used highlighted on hover and scrolled into view). There is no step picking: on a wrong answer the learner writes one correction (not told which step is wrong), the judge reads it like teaching (it updates Kai's notebook for the rest of practice), the notebook strikes through what no longer holds in pencil and writes the new notes in a second ink, and Kai tries again. Not prewritten variants: misconceptions arise while explaining. Without a key, or if the model fails, `offlineSolve` builds Kai's attempt from the worked solution, breaking at the first step whose fact the notebook lacks or has wrong (logged as `by: offline`).
Worked solutions come on the results page after the final test, in both conditions. **Every step is timed; in the pilot the times are recommendations (2026-11-04).** Each step has a time on Study setup (`pretest.minutes` 8 per set, `watch.minutes` 12, `teach.minutes` 15, `practice.minutes` 20 from practice.json, `test.minutes` 20 per form; raised after the first try-out, whose limits were far too short), stated up front (the welcome page's intro and cards, each page's lede) and shown as a clock in the header (`components/StepClock.tsx`: `useDeadline`, the start kept with the session: `pretestStartedAt`, `readStartedAt`, `chat.startedAt`, `practice.startedAt`, `testStartedAt`). The pretest and the test have a clock per part (a set, a form: `pretestPartAt`, `testPartAt`), each with the full time. `timing.soft` (default on, the pilot): the clock counts down, then on past the time in amber ("+2:10"), and nothing ends on its own: learners take as long as they need (Kai's fixed questions don't wrap up at the time, and the chat route doesn't refuse late messages). Each step's and part's time against its time is logged (`stepTime`: `step_time` with `ms`, `limitMs`, `diffMs`; the pretest's and the test's parts also go with the answers, `times` in the `pretest` and `grade` events) and exported as `time<Part>Min`, `time<Part>RecMin`, `time<Part>DiffMin` (Pretest1, Pretest2, Watch, Teach, Practice, Test1, Test2; `timeColumns` in `lib/analytics.ts`), on the dashboard ("Time against the recommended time") and on each session's page, to set the study's times and adjust the items. Soft off: hard limits, and the step ends itself (the part is handed in as it is, unanswered recorded as null, `timedOut`; the lesson closes; Kai wraps up, `teach_time_up`, and the chat route refuses a message 90 s past it; practice goes on to the test). Export columns `pretestTimedOut`, `pretestUnanswered`, `readTimedOut`, `teachTimedOut`, `testTimedOut`. Teaching ends after Kai's last question (or at a hard limit, or "I've taught all I can"); the header shows how far Kai has got as a plain count ("3 / 10"), and nothing on the participant pages says the questions are a fixed list (it would change how learners see Kai). About 103 minutes in the pilot (both pretest sets and test forms) plus the questions after the test.

**Debug skip (2026-11-04).** A session started with the shared code (`ACCESS_CODE`; not team or trial codes made in the admin panel, not participants) gets `debug` from `/api/session` and a "Skip to …" button in the corner of every step (`components/DebugSkip.tsx`): the next part of the pretest or test, else the next step, without waiting for a clock or answering everything. Logged as `debug_skip` (export `debugSkips`); a skipped pretest or test is graded as it is and marked `skipped`.

**Build to one level.** Both conditions run end to end at MVP depth; improve them together rather than polishing one far ahead of the other. Dead code (unused functions, fields) can go; whole tools that cost effort to build or whose outputs took effort to generate are stashed, not deleted (the user's rule, 2026-10-08), like the authoring pipeline in `content/authoring/` with its outputs.

**Consent.** The welcome page has a checkbox with the study note; Start stays off until it is ticked, and `/api/session` refuses a session without `consent: true` (logged as `consent`, a version date; change `CONSENT_VERSION` with the wording).

**The follow-up (delayed) test** (2026-10-20). Two test forms, A (t01–t10) and B (v01–v10, harder): the team's post-test (below); a pack's form B may instead hold twins of form A's items (`pair`). A session's form is in its token; new sessions alternate A and B within each condition (`nextForm`), so each condition gets both orders equally often. Setting `test.followUp` (on/off) and `test.followUpDays` (opens on its own this many days after the final test; 0: right away), on Study setup and on the admin panel's Follow-up page (`DELAYED_TEST_DAYS` sets a fresh install's default; older saved settings where 0 days meant off are migrated). For a code that names one person (a one-use participant or trial code, or an older HMAC participant code) the server keeps a record (`lib/server/participants.ts`: condition, form, topic, when each test was taken, and `opensAt` when the admin opened it early; Upstash or `.data/participants.json`; `followUpOpens` is the one rule: the schedule, or earlier if opened). Grading the final test returns `followUp.opensAt`, so the results page invites the person back with the date, and the welcome page offers "Take the follow-up test" on that device (the server decides; too early gives "opens on …"). Coming back with the same code (checked before counting a use) or the device button: a session of its own (`phase: delayed`, `openedEarly` logged) with only the test, in the other form, asking first whether the learner has studied the topic since (`since`); after both, "done both". Follow-up page (`/admin/follow-up`, `/api/admin/followup`): the switch and days, counts (waiting, open, done, still in the first session), everyone with a record, "Open now" and "Back to schedule" per person and "Open now for everyone waiting" (participants, trials or both). To try it: Study setup's "Try it as a participant", take the final test, Open now on Follow-up, back to the welcome page. Sessions last `SESSION_DAYS` (10). The test questions come per session from `/api/grade` (`kind: items`, its own form only), never with the page. The dashboard keeps follow-up sessions out of the funnel and the main numbers and summarises them apart. Not after a final test with both forms (the pilot, `test.both`): nothing unseen is left, so the follow-up can't be on while both forms are (`settingsProblem`), the person's record says `both`, and the Follow-up page leaves them out.

**A study is a topic pack plus blocks (2026-10-15).** Nothing about bandits or this study's shape is built into the app; the bandits study is one setup.
- **Topic packs** (`lib/server/topics.ts`, registry; `lib/server/content.ts`, `packFor(slug)`): everything a topic brings, from `topics/<slug>/`: bundle, practice, exercises (forms), pretest, survey, coverage and transcript chunks, and `copy.json` (the participant-facing words: subject, welcome headline, Kai's first line, what to watch for, the correction placeholder, the page description). Static imports (nothing read from disk at run time). A session's token carries its `topic`: it keeps it if the study switches. Routes use the session's pack, pages the study's current one (`currentStudy()` in `lib/server/study.ts`). Adding a topic: its files, `pnpm content ingest|validate|bundle <slug>`, one line in the registry. The schemas of the files the app serves (test forms, pretest, survey, copy) live in `content/pack.ts`, shared by the server and `pnpm content validate`, which checks the whole pack (`checkPack`: every file as the server parses it, practice `needs` against the bundle's facts, matching test forms and twins, transcripts per video, the registry line). `DOCENDO_TOPIC` sets a fresh install's pack.
- **Blocks** (`lib/flow.ts`, client-safe): pretest, watch, teach, practice, exercises (the final test), results, in that order; pretest, watch and practice can be switched off (teaching, the test and the results always run), plus the questions after the test. `flowOf(settings)` is the session's steps; `nextStep`, `currentStep`, `reached` drive every page's "continue" and the guard (`useGuard`, `useNext`); the header's bars and the welcome page's step cards come from the flow (colours by step: watch red, teach yellow, practice green, test blue). A step switched off mid-session sends saved sessions past it.
- **Settings** (`lib/settings.ts`, nested per block: `topic`, `studyLine`, `pretest.on`, `watch.{on,parts}`, `teach.{stopAfterMessages,stopAfterMinutes,fixed,mind}`, `practice.{on,minutes,tries}`, `test.{followUp,followUpDays,both}`, `survey.on`, `sessionDays`, `tools.{calculator,voice,help}`, `dailyTurnLimit`). `lib/server/settings.ts`: Upstash `docendo:settings:v1` or `.data/settings.json`, 10 s cache (admin pages read `fresh`), merged block by block over the defaults (env and pack); a version saved in the older flat shape is migrated (`migrateSettings`). Each version has an id (hash); saving logs `settings_change`; `session_start` logs `settings`, `topic` and the flow. `PublicSettings` (flow, timings, tools, the pack's copy, the survey or null) reach the client through `SettingsContext`. The admin route refuses what the pack can't do (a follow-up test or both forms with one form, both forms with a follow-up, practice without problems). Every number the admin panel or the participant pages show about the study (parts, minutes, rounds, problems, tries, test questions and parts, survey questions, Kai's readiness rule from `policy.ts`, the score charts' range) is counted from the pack, the settings or the engine (`packSummary`; counts in words through `lib/words.ts`), never written down. A switched-off step leaves no trace: practice has no Rewatch without the video step, and the dashboard drops a skipped step's empty rows. What defines Kai or the test's structure (readiness, wrap-up, problems, forms, conditions, consent) is shown read-only as "fixed by design", counted from the code and the pack.

**After the final test, before the score** (`components/Survey.tsx`, `topics/bandits/survey.json`): six questions, the same in both conditions, optional: effort, responsibility for Kai, judged understanding, practice helped, enjoyment (1 to 7), and the manipulation check `lights` (the practice's light was about my own understanding / Kai's / not sure). Logged as `client_survey`, columns `survey*`, a dashboard card per condition. Not after the follow-up test.

**Conditions come from participant codes.** Each participant gets their own access code; an HMAC of the code under `CODE_SECRET` gives a check part and the condition bit (`lib/codes.ts`), so the server keeps no table or counter. `pnpm codes 40` writes balanced blocks of 4 to `data/codes-*.csv` (team only). The shared `ACCESS_CODE` is for the team and demos: direct condition, test shortcuts on (`team` in the browser). The token carries `cond`, `entry` (participant, team, open) and `pid` (the code's id); the browser gets `team` and `condition` (never displayed). Participants practise in their condition (the server refuses Kai's solving to direct participants); team sessions get a "You solve / Kai solves" switch in the practice header to see both.

**Teammates' proposals:** judge them on their merits. Don't adopt one because it is more deterministic: keep the AI for judging and Kai's reasoning, and use code for the checks around it.

## The app, in four chapters

0. **Before you start** (`/pretest`, `topics/bandits/pretest.json`, rules in `content/pretest.ts`): the team's option C (2026-11-02), about 4 minutes. Three screening questions: an RL course or bandits covered in a course (q1) and being able to explain an algorithm such as UCB (q2) flag the session `excluded` (it continues); a probability or statistics course (q3) is only recorded. Then a set of five knowledge items, each with "I don't know" and no written reason: two on the maths the lesson builds on (√n, ln t) and three on everyday statistical reasoning behind the post-test, with no algorithm named. Set C1 is the default; C2 is a harder version of the same five (`pair`). Pilot (`pretest.both`, Study setup): every set, one after the other as parts, in an order that alternates with the session's test form (A: C1 first), the part before locked once the learner goes on; the sets are fixed when the page first opens. Graded and logged by `/api/grade` (`kind: "pretest"`, answers by item id with the sets in order; the older array form still works): the score in all and per set, "I don't know" answers, `excluded`, the content's version. Nothing is shown back; no step bar.
1. **Watch** (`/watch`): one lesson of 8:12, three parts of two YouTube videos played back to back in one player (YouTube IFrame API; plain embeds as fallback). **With a text lesson** (`topics/<slug>/lesson.json`, 2026-11-02; the bandits pack has one) the same step reads it instead, called "Read" (`stepName`; the welcome page's card and Kai's lines say so too): five chapters, shown one at a time like slides (2026-11-03: a long page invited scrolling past): a bar of chapters on top (the ones read can be opened again, the ones ahead can't), "Next" at the end of the chapter, opening after a quick read of it (`chapterSeconds`: about 400 words a minute, at least 8 s; the chapter's start is kept with the session, `readChapterAt`, `readAt`, `readMax`), with three formulas (MathML, `components/lesson/Formula.tsx`) and five figures (plain SVG, `components/lesson/Figures.tsx`, drawn with the team). It states every teachable fact once and none of the consequences the practice and test items ask about (a test guards a few phrases). Against reciting it to Kai: the text comes with the page (never in the browser's code) and can't be selected or copied (attempts logged); the last chapter's "teach Kai" opens after `minSeconds` (240, kept with the session so a reload doesn't restart it) and a reminder shows after `remindSeconds` (720); once the learner moves on the page stays closed (team sessions may look again); the teaching chat refuses pasting; a message sharing 15 or more consecutive words with the text gets Kai's fixed reply (`copied`: say it in your own words) and nothing is judged or written down; every chat turn logs its overlap with the text (the longest shared run, and its words in shared runs of 8 or more; `lib/lesson.ts`). Logged per chapter (`read_chapter`: seconds, where to; `read_continue` also has `chapters` and `chapterSec`). "ln" is set in a serif face wherever it appears (`withLn`, `.ln`; in the body font its l read as an I to a pilot participant). Help (Kai's card, practice's Reread; only with `tools.help` on, off by default: Study setup's "Help from the lesson") shows the section about the idea in question (`nodes` per section), as text that can't be selected, once the learner has had a go.
2. **Teach Kai** (`/teach`): a chat until Kai feels ready, Kai asking its fixed questions (`script.json`, below) or choosing its own. Kai's mind (`/mind` and a toggle) shows what Kai has understood.
3. **Practice** (`/practice`): two rounds of three problems, one at a time, two tries each (the learner's, or Kai's with one reteach in between), Rewatch for help, 10 minutes, then straight to the test.
4. **Final test** (`/exercises`, then `/results`): one of two forms (A or B, below), or in the pilot both (`test.both`, fixed per session in its token): two parts, the session's own form first, so the order alternates with the form within each condition; part 2 opens once part 1 is complete and part 1 is then locked; the anchor (v09 = t10, `same`) is asked once and counts for both forms. The team's post-test (2026-11-02): form A = T1 to T10, form B = V1 to V10 (harder: changed rules, an inverse item, three select-all-that-apply items scored all or nothing). Every item offers "I don't know" (graded wrong) and asks for the reason in one or two sentences; a question counts as answered with a reason of a few words (`lib/answers.ts`), and the reason field calculates like the working field. Each form's items come in an order of the learner's own (`lib/order.ts`: seeded from the session, so a reload keeps it; logged as `test_order` and per item `pos`; `client_test_part` when part 1 is done), graded on the server (`content/test.ts`). Every graded test logs a fingerprint of each form's content (`testVersion`, per item `version`), and the dashboard's per-item numbers count only the current version (an edited item keeps its id; the export keeps every answer with its version). The grade event logs the forms, each form's score (`formScores`, the anchor in both), "I don't know" answers and every reason. The results page asks for feedback right under the score (in the pilot also the score per part), then reviews the missed questions first ("Missed / All"), each with the learner's reason. Practice keeps its fixed order: it is a learning sequence, round 2 mirrors round 1, and in the recursive condition a correction carries into the next problems. The results show the explanations and the practice solutions.

**Lesson scope ("How strategies choose", 2026-10-08):** one goal G, "predict which option greedy, ε-greedy and UCB pick next and explain why greedy can lock in while ε-greedy and UCB keep exploring". `topic.yaml` → `lesson` holds the goal, the 9 nodes (n01, n03, n05, n06, n07, n08, n16, n11, n12), facts made optional here (n03.f2, n06.f2, n08.f2, n16.f2) and the video segments. **The facts follow the team's fact list (F1 to F15, 2026-11-02):** given facts Kai knows and the problems' stems repeat (the setup n01.f1 F1, n01.f2 F2; averages n03.f1 and n16.f1 F3; trying each option once n03.f3 F15; an untried option's unlimited bonus n11.f3 F12), never in a problem's `needs`; and eight teachable ones, each with Kai's `assume` (its misconception when not taught): greedy follows the leader and switches (n05.f1 F4), lock-in (n06.f1 F5, without "a too-high average corrects itself", which T1 and V1 ask learners to work out), a fixed ε early and late (n07.f1 F6), a random pick among all options, the favourite included (n07.f2 F7), UCB's score and the highest one (n11.f1 F9), c sets the bonus's size and the bonus ignores the observed rewards (n11.f2 F14), own picks shrink the bonus (n12.f1 F10), the bonus grows with the round number, ever more slowly (n12.f2 F11, also `assumeVague`). The benefit of the doubt (n11.f4 F13) is text only. `pnpm content bundle` builds `bundle.json` (what the app reads) from that slice: 13 facts Kai waits for, help timestamps clipped to the segments, and `outOfScopeTerms` (lexicon of the other nodes, e.g. "regret") that Kai may only use after the learner does. `graph.draft.json` (17 core and 2 branch nodes, goals LG1–LG3 from the C2 document) stays the full source. The bundle holds all of them: the 9 lesson ideas (`inLesson`) and, around them, the 10 others (`inLesson: false`: no goals, nothing required or teachable, no help). Kai's mind shows the whole tree with the outside ideas dimmed; Kai never asks about them (the policy only opens ideas with teachable facts, and never contradicts outside ones) and they never count, but the judge sees all facts, so a learner who explains one gets it noted as bonus.

## App rules (do not break)

- **Nothing secret in git or the browser.** `.env*` (except `.env.example`), `.data/` and `data/` are ignored; the history was scanned 2026-10-14 (no keys, tokens, filled-in secrets or research data). Public pages never name env variables. CSV exports neutralise cells starting with = + - @ (formula injection from learners' text). Wrong access codes: 10 per 15 minutes per network, then 429.
- **Secrets stay on the server.** `OPENROUTER_API_KEY`, `SESSION_SECRET`, `CODE_SECRET`, Upstash credentials, test and pretest answers and practice solutions are only read in `lib/server/*` (marked `server-only`) and API routes. Never `NEXT_PUBLIC_`. After a build, `.next/static` must contain no key, answer, solution or lesson fact text.
- **The browser never calls OpenRouter.** Only `/api/session` (also the delayed test's start: `followUp`), `/api/chat`, `/api/practice`, `/api/grade` (also a session's test questions: `kind: items`), `/api/log`, `/api/transcribe` (voice input: a 16 kHz WAV of at most 2 minutes, token in a header; live pieces 45 a minute per session, counted locally; final passes 8 a minute and 60 a session, against the daily cap; refused in demo mode; the audio is not kept) and the admin routes `/api/admin/*` (login, logout, codes, export, settings; all but login need the admin cookie) exist. Each validates input with zod, caps sizes, checks the Origin, requires a signed session token (except `/api/session`), and is rate-limited (`lib/server/limit.ts`).
- **Limits are per learner first.** A lab room shares one IP, so per-IP caps stay generous (120 chat turns/min). Per-session caps do the real work (10/min, 45 per session), plus a site-wide daily cap. Only the checks that protect the model budget (chat per session, the site-wide daily cap, which Kai's solving also counts against) and the once-per-session ones (session start, grading) go to Upstash; bursts, `/api/practice` and `/api/log` count in memory (`"local"` in `limitAll`): each shared check costs about 5 Redis commands.
- **No key means demo mode** (`config.demo`): an offline judge and writer, with a visible "Demo mode" badge. The whole flow must keep working without a key. If the key runs out of credits mid-study (OpenRouter 402), a chat turn falls back to the offline judge and writer (logged `degraded`) and Kai's solving to the offline attempt; keep a credit buffer on the key.
- **Kai's state lives in the browser** (localStorage) and is re-validated on every request (`engine/tutor/state.ts`). Tampering only affects that learner's own session.
- **Engine stays pure TypeScript** in `engine/tutor/` (no Next imports), so it can be unit-tested and reused by a CLI. The browser imports only its schema-free parts: `config.ts` (Kai's rules, `CONFIG`, and `MESSAGE_MAX`) and `notebook.ts` (`notesOf`, `noteParts`, `quotesOf`, `latest`, `activeMisconceptions`); everything else as `import type`. `state.ts` and `policy.ts` re-export them. So zod and the engine stay out of the participant bundle (2026-10-18: one 109 KB chunk less).
- **Kai's writer and solver never see lesson facts**, only the notebook (learner quotes and misconceptions still believed: a later correct statement of the fact it contradicts retires a misconception, `activeMisconceptions`; one message that teaches a fact and contradicts it leaves it partial and the belief set aside), plus the move and recent chat (writer) or the problem (solver). The solver's steps are term-checked and leaks logged (`leaked`), not rewritten. Leak control is the term check in `engine/tutor/admit.ts` (one rewrite, then a fallback line).
- **Every judge verdict quotes the learner word for word.** Code drops verdicts and misconceptions whose quote isn't in the message (`findQuote`: exact up to case and punctuation, or ≥ 80% of ≥ 3 quote words in order and close together; the notebook stores the learner's matching span, never the judge's wording). The judge sometimes quotes the fact itself instead of the learner: then the learner's own sentence that shares at least 60% (and 4) of the quote's distinctive words stands in (`sentenceLike`), else the verdict is dropped (2026-11-05: an expert's c was lost twice that way). The lesson's facts come first in the prompt and the ideas around it after, credited only when a message clearly goes beyond every lesson fact ("ε stays the same" had gone to a decaying-ε idea outside the lesson). Each teachable fact has a `key` (what makes it correct rather than partial; generous about wording, not about the key), and where the judge still overlooks it an `evidence` pattern in code (n12.f2's slowing growth, n07.f1's fixed rate): a "correct" verdict for a message without it becomes "partial" (logged among the dropped). The judge's JSON schema restricts ids to real facts and nodes (it once returned "n01.f1: <text>", which silently dropped every verdict).
- **Kai's notes are whole statements, the learner's words first** (2026-10-17; one module, `engine/tutor/notes.ts`: `annotate` tries, in order, the quote as it stands (begins a sentence of the learner's, six words or more, doesn't point back with "it", "that", "nope"…), the learner's whole sentence around a fragment (unless longer than 240 characters, or it would take in a part recorded the other way), the quote without a leading "so"/"and"; `state.ts`: `note`, `noteParts`, `quotesOf`; tests in `tests/notes.test.ts`). Only where the message has nothing more (a short answer to Kai's question: "between arms", "nope, never") does the judge's `before`/`after` add a few words, assembled in code around the verbatim quote and kept only if they add no lesson term the learner or Kai's question didn't use, no number, at most two new content words and none after the quote, and don't repeat the quote; otherwise the bare quote. ## Kai's loop (engine/tutor)

`judge` (LLM, or mock) → `applyJudgement` (notebook, misconceptions, used terms, attempts, unparking) → `nextMove` (policy) → `applyMove` → `write` (LLM, or mock) → `forbiddenTerms` check → reply.

**Kai's questions (`topics/<slug>/script.json`, `engine/tutor/script.ts`; rebuilt on the graph 2026-11-05 after the pilot).** When the pack has a script and `teach.fixed` is on, every word Kai asks is in the file, the same for everyone; only which question comes next is chosen, by a fixed rule in code over the notebook (logged per turn), never by a model. The pilot showed why: Phil's colleague's ten questions didn't map onto the facts the practice gate checks (an expert's 10/10 learner got 7 of 13 facts credited and Kai solved nothing; "kept asking questions I had already answered"; the own-example opener cost minutes). Now: Kai's opening states the setup itself (given facts); one question per idea in the graph's order (greedy, where greedy ends up, ε-greedy, UCB, what sets the bonus, UCB over many rounds), then "anything else?". Each question names the teachable facts it is there for (`targets`, every teachable fact has one): a question whose facts are already answered (taught, or answered with a wrong belief about that fact, which Kai believes) is skipped (`covered`), and after an answer each target still missing or vague gets its own fixed follow-up (`follow`, once); a short complete answer gets none ("say more" only for questions without targets). Follow-ups are open questions that name no answer (no yes/no between the rule and its wrong reading, no scenario that gives the point away; lock-in has none). Kai never asks a question naming a lesson term the learner hasn't used (the term check of Kai's free replies) apart from the names Kai knows from the slides (`known`: the topic's and the rules' names), so the bonus questions come once the learner has talked about UCB's bonus; such a question waits at the back (`gated`) and is asked after the last one if the term has come up by then. A session saved under another version of the questions (its `ids` differ, e.g. across a deploy) carries on from the first question not yet answered (`restart`). A question to Kai or a reply that isn't an answer gets the question again, once; still none, it moves on and comes back once at the end if its facts are still missing ("I don't know" is an answer: no coming back). Before the question Kai reacts in its own words (`llmReact` in `writer.ts`, the writer model): it says back, in a few of the learner's words, what it got, or that it isn't sure it follows yet, never what it is missing or a case of its own, never a verdict; no question of its own (code drops any sentence with "?"; a decimal point doesn't end a sentence; an echo over 32 words is cut to its first sentence); term-checked like Kai's free replies, a fixed line after two failures or a timeout. A message that only taught a misconception leaves Kai content. Kai wraps up after the last question, at the first message after `minutes`, or after 30 messages, and reads its notes back. The header shows a plain count ("3 / 7") and nothing on the participant pages calls the questions a fixed list. Logged per turn as `script` (question answered, the next one, `probe`, `covered`, `back`, `reaction`; export columns `scriptQuestion`, `scriptProbe`, `scriptCovered`, `scriptGated`, `scriptRestart`, `scriptBack`, `reaction`, and per turn `misconceptionFacts` and `judgeChecks`). `pnpm content validate` checks targets and follow-ups and the terms each question may name. **`pnpm eval:sim`** (scripts/eval-sim.ts) tests it end to end: simulated learners (a model role-playing a student with a fixed knowledge sheet: expert, solid, terse, partial, misled, realistic, rambler, chatty) talk to the real Kai with the live judge and writer, and the report gives facts credited against what each knows, false credit, vague answers credited as full, wrong beliefs caught, questions the learner felt they had already answered, messages and words, whether the practice gate opens exactly where the learner's knowledge suffices, and (`--solve`) Kai's practice from that notebook with one correction each; `--empty` starts practice from an empty notebook (the corrections' test). 2026-11-05, 2 × 8 learners × 2 learner models (Claude Haiku and Gemini 2.5 Flash playing the learner): recall 98/98 both, false credit 0, vague as full 0, wrong beliefs 15/16, repeats 6 and 7 in 16 conversations, gate 96/96 both; baseline (the old questions) 26/30 gate, 11 repeats in 5 conversations, twice the messages. The older `eval:teach` is stashed in `scripts/stash/` (its scripted answers fit the old question order).

- Every required fact has an `ask`, Kai's novice question for it (ancestors' terms only). Moves: open (the idea's `probes.open`), followUp (the next missing fact's `ask`; asked once, or twice after a partial or wrong answer; "I don't know" skips on; an off-topic reply re-asks without using it up), nudge ("what's next?" before the idea is done: one "before we move on…", then park), deepen (one probe per explained idea), misconception (Kai voices the idea's common misconception as its own guess; not for the root idea, where it only derailed a beginner), contradict (a new wrong fact), answer (the learner asked Kai; carries the next question in `then`), wrapUp. `listen` stays in the enum only so old saved states parse. `closure` makes Kai say "got it" or "let's come back to it" before the next question. All questions used up → the idea is parked; a correct verdict on any of its facts unparks it.
- Policy order (`policy.ts`): wrap up if ready or at 30 messages → answer the learner's question → contradict a new wrong fact → move_on: nudge once, then park → focus explained: misconception, deepen, move on → next missing fact's question → park and move on. Next idea: 10 × unlocked + 3 × mentioned now + 2 × serves the least-covered goal + 1 × child of focus − 2 × parked; ties by graph order.
- The judge also returns `misconceptions`: any wrong belief, open-ended (no fixed list or ids), with the idea's node id and the lesson fact it contradicts (`fact`, or none; only that fact's need is blocked in practice, so a true remark beyond the facts, once read as wrong, no longer shuts the gate; a later or same-message correct statement of that fact retires it; code keeps the fact only when the quote is about it, `isAbout`: it shares a word with the fact, its `assume` or `key`, or its idea's terms, else none, after the judge pinned UCB's bonus fact on a sentence about ε-greedy; a wrong detail the fact doesn't speak about, like "shrinks linearly", isn't a contradiction, and a formula or worked example that shows a fact credits it, like "the favourite gets 1 − ε + ε/k"), a verbatim quote and a one-line `belief` for the log only. A message that mixes a right and a wrong part gets both: the right part credited, the wrong part recorded (the prompt says so explicitly; without it the right half was sometimes lost, which wrongly closed the practice gate). That is across facts; within one fact a contradicted part makes it "wrong" (2026-11-03: "greedy picks whatever looked best after the first day and then stays there" was credited as greedy's rule, which says it switches; `pnpm eval:judge` false credit 2 → 0 over 3 runs). Still generous with vague answers ("sometimes random", "greedy goes with the best one" credited as correct in `eval:teach`): showing the judge each fact's assumption fixed one and lost two clear ones, so it was left out. `state.misconceptions` keeps the learner's words; Kai believes them.
- Intents: explain, answer, ask_kai, unsure, move_on, off_topic; only explain, answer and unsure count as an attempt (unlocks help). One reading per message (`consistent` in `judge.ts`): a message classed unsure, off_topic or move_on credits no fact and records no misconception (its verdicts are dropped and logged), so Kai's "let's leave that part" and its notebook agree. A short "no" counts when it answers Kai's question ("nope, never"); one that only says they don't know ("nope, not this either" after "i dunno") is unsure (2026-10-20: the judge had read it both ways and credited a fact).
- Ready (`readyReason`): ≥ 80% of the teachable required facts, or ≥ 50% once the learner has taught for 8 minutes; every goal needs an explained idea. Wrap-up after 30 learner messages regardless. Partial counts ½ and never overwrites a correct verdict.
- Moods per Kai message: great, okay, confused (`moodFor`), plus neutral and thinking in the UI. Kai's mind (`mind.ts`, `MindView.tsx`): statuses off, bonus, unseen, mentioned ("Started"), explained ("Done"), checked, parked ("Set aside"). One cue per meaning: the fill says how far Kai got (white, dark outline, pale green, solid green with a tick, amber with a pause), dots are facts, a blue "now" tag is the idea Kai is on, a dark "new" tag what changed since the learner last left the view (`chat.mindSeen`; one line above the graph names the changes, `mind_changes`), blue outline only the selection. Ideas around the lesson are faint dotted outlines (green when explained anyway: bonus). No ids on the boxes; goal shapes (violet, orange, teal, never red, yellow or green) only when a lesson has several goals. Below the graph, full width: the idea and its state left, Kai's questions with the learner's words right (one quote under questions it answered together). The view opens at its top; on phones the graph scrolls sideways, starting at the idea Kai is on.

## Content

- Written by hand: `graph.draft.json` (nodes with `id`, `label`, `needs`, `goals`, `facts` with `required` and `ask`, `lexicon`, `misconception`, `probes`), `questions.json` (contradictions, fallbacks), `coverage.json` (which facts the learner videos state, with chunk ids; a deliberate gap gets `accepted`), `practice.json`, `exercises.json`, `pretest.json`.
- **Authoring pipeline, stashed** in `content/authoring/`: not part of the MVP flow, kept for building new topics later. `draft` (an LLM drafts the graph and questions), `locate` (BM25 candidates per fact → `locations.json`, kept; its top 3 held the right passage for 19 of 26 facts, better than a bge-small embedding at 16), `verify` (an LLM decides coverage), `review` (team review page), `freeze` (graph.json with a hash; `bundle` reads graph.json if present, else graph.draft.json). The reference sources in topic.yaml (ritvikmath's UCB video, Sutton & Barto and Slivkins PDFs) feed only these steps; their chunks are kept. The bandits content itself was written by hand.
- `pnpm content ingest` fetches every source in topic.yaml (YouTube captions through the player API as the Android client, or a `<sourceId>.vtt` in `sources/raw/`; PDF and web pages for the reference sources). `validate` checks structure and leak-safety: a probe's `open` and every `ask` may use only ancestors' lexicon terms; `why`/`whatIf`/`compute`, misconception lines and contradiction questions may also use the node's own terms; fallback lines none (`hasTerm` in `content/terms.ts`, also used by `admit`). `bundle` slices it for the app.
- Graph layout (`content/layout.ts`, Kai's mind): layered on a grid, long edges routed through lanes, crossings minimised; tests guard it. Columns are 196 px (a 52 px gap) so links curve gently, and each link runs straight for its last 12 px, so the arrowhead (a rounded triangle along the path's end, coloured like its line) points the way the line arrives. Hovering an idea lights its links in soft blue; the selected idea's links are strong blue.
- **Practice** (`practice.json`, `content/practice.ts`): six problems in two rounds (`round`), the team's P1 to P6 (2026-11-02): festival stages (ε-greedy), exercise types (UCB), two cakes (greedy and UCB); audio tours (UCB), page layouts (ε-greedy and UCB; needs n11.f2 as well since 2026-11-03: "A or B" holds only because the bonus sizes are set by c, and without it a Kai with full notes answered "only B"), thumbnails (ε-greedy and UCB over many rounds). Round 2 repeats round 1's facts in new settings (`idea`: epsilon, ucb, compare). Options state only the prediction, and every wrong option is the prediction of a named misconception (Kai's assumption for a fact, or a known learner one), so none can be ruled out without the facts. Kinds `choice` ("pick and why": every option pairs a choice with a reason) or `number` (tolerance; "85%", "0,85" and "85" read as 0.85 for a probability). Every solution step lists the `facts` it uses; `needs` lists the facts without which the answer can't be reached (the gate; "a|b" when either fact carries the rule, e.g. UCB's "average plus bonus, pick the highest" is credited as n11.f1 or n11.f2), and `beliefs` the beginner's wrong rules per needed fact with the answer each leads to (Kai's guesses). `/api/practice` checks (at most `PRACTICE_TRIES`, default 2; a non-number doesn't use a try) and finds Rewatch moments (`lib/server/moments.ts`); `/api/grade` returns the solutions with the graded test. `minutes` (or `PRACTICE_MINUTES`) sets the timer, which survives reloads. "The bonus grows with t" (n12.f2) and c (n11.f2) are not in the videos: their coverage is `accepted` because the text lesson states them.
- **Final test** (`exercises.json`, schema in `content/pack.ts`, rules in `content/test.ts`): the team's post-test, form A (t01 to t10, version 1) and form B (v01 to v10, version 2, harder). Every item has `dontKnow` and `reason`, a `rubric` for the reason (2 points with both named elements, 1 with one), a `diagnosis` per option (correct, or the misconception it reflects: Kai's assumption for a fact, or a known learner one), an `estimate` (the team's guess of the share correct, to compare with the pilot), and a `title` and `note` for the team; none of these reach the browser. Kinds `choice`, `multi` (select all that apply: `answer` lists the true statements, right only if exactly those are ticked) and `number` (with `unit` and `tolerance`, graded by `checkNumber`); a `table` with a `question` under it where an item needs one (T9's two logs). `same` marks an item identical to one of the other form (v09 = t10, the anchor); `pnpm content validate` checks that it is. Rules: settings of their own (never the lesson's or the practice's), options that state predictions only, every wrong option a named misconception, keys spread over positions and never the longest option. `tests/posttest.test.ts` checks the keys against the team's answer key and recomputes the numbers behind them.

A hairline only where space alone doesn't separate (between long questions, page head and work); not under a heading or above a block that starts with its own title. Descriptions use the column's width with `text-wrap: pretty`, never a cap in characters.
- One working field (`components/Scratchpad.tsx`, sums by `lib/calc.ts`: no eval) wherever a learner writes: the working in direct practice, the correction to Kai, under each final test question. Type a sum and "=" and the result is written after it, like a notes calculator; the line being typed shows its value underneath; "Use … as the answer" for number problems. A key row (× ÷ √ ^ …) is closed by default everywhere and remembered per browser. The same tool in both conditions: the study measures the rules, not the arithmetic.
- Kai's notebook: white paper with blue rules and a red margin line, the writing sitting on the rules, a faint second sheet offset (not rotated: rotation overflowed phones), notes in Shantell Sans (an informal hand that still belongs with Bricolage and Figtree), navy ink, margin numbers; struck in pencil and rewritten in green ink after a correction. No spiral rings or other props.
- The recursive practice view (page up to 1240 px): the task on the left (problem, options, Kai's tries, Rewatch), Kai's notebook on the right, sticky, scrolling inside the paper, one height (`clamp(300px, 100dvh − 470px, 720px)`) so the first screen fits without scrolling and the correction box adds height below instead of squeezing it. The correction is an ordinary text box under the notebook, shown only when Kai can be corrected, and scrolled into view: the notebook is Kai's, the learner tells Kai and Kai writes down what it takes. On phones: task, notebook, correction.
- Voice input (`components/Dictation.tsx`, `lib/client/dictation.ts`, `public/voice-worklet.js`): a mic beside Send in the chat and in the correction box. While the learner speaks: a pill with a live waveform, the time, ✕ (discard, Esc) and ✓ (done); the audio since the last settled point (a rolling window up to about 8 s) is written out again about every 2.5 s (`/api/transcribe`, kind preview, one request at a time) and shown in the box in grey italics over the typed text, improving as the sentence goes on; a window of 4 s or more settles at the next pause. On ✓ only the open window is read once more (kind final); the settled parts stand, and the learner edits before sending. Every clip is trimmed in the browser (quiet at both ends cut, pauses shortened to 0.4 s): about half the audio to pay for, and no silence for a model to fill. Guards against invented text: silence is never sent, the prompt is plain (no topic: told the topic, models made up bandit sentences from silence and pulled mishearings towards the topic, a claim the judge could credit), and a transcript longer than the audio could hold (`plausibleWords`) is dropped. About $0.003 a minute of speech, so a spoken answer costs about as much as the chat turn it feeds. Hidden in demo mode.
- Welcome page: exactly one screen on laptops and larger (no scroll bar), three zones; the Start area keeps one height in every state (loading, code field, continue), so nothing jumps. Ending teaching shows Kai's closing line in place; the learner moves on with a click. Learner messages up to 4,000 characters.

## Terminology and writing

- Say **condition** (direct / recursive feedback) or **option**. Never "arm" for a study condition; "arm" is only the bandit term.
- Kai = the tutee; learner = the human who teaches.
- No " · " separators in text, UI, titles, docs or console output (it reads as AI-generated); use ordinary punctuation. Few decorative symbols; prefer × in maths.
