## What to run, and what to look for

1. **The judge**, `pnpm eval:judge --reps 3`
   - False credit stays 0, and every case from the pilots passes (8b906ca, 8502ddf, d0356f3).
   - In the dropped verdicts, look for "correct → partial: no evidence of the key" or "no words stating the key" on a case that wants "correct". That means a check is too strict.
2. **Teaching end to end**, `pnpm eval:sim --reps 2 --solve --show` (about $0.02 a conversation, 8 simulated learners)
   - Compare with the 2026-11-05 numbers in CLAUDE.md. Recall will drop for the terse and partial learners; that is intended.
   - What matters:
     - credit matches what the learner actually said;
     - false credit and "vague as full" stay 0;
     - wrong beliefs are caught;
     - few repeats (Kai asking what was already answered);
     - the gate opens exactly where the learner's knowledge suffices.
   - In practice:
     - Kai is wrong at least once in most sessions ("NEVER WRONG" rare), from a gap or a caveat;
     - one correction sets it right;
     - a misreading never goes against a note.
   - Read the reactions: each should say back the latest message only.
3. **Corrections from scratch**, `pnpm eval:sim --empty --reps 1`
4. **The solver**, `pnpm eval:solver --items p7,p3,p2 --reps 10 --audit`
   - A full notebook gets at least 9 of 10 right; without each needed fact, Kai lands on that fact's wrong answer at least 8 of 10 times.
5. **By hand**, `pnpm chat` (live), two conversations:
   - one terse, like the two terse runs: lock-in should be partial, "for exploring" partial;
   - one thorough, like `P_TestAfterFixes1`: everything credited, including the lucky-leader remark, with no misconception on n05.f1.

## When something fails

Fix it yourself, in the spirit of CLAUDE.md:
- the AI judges and Kai reasons;
- code checks around them;
- the judge stays generous about wording, not about the key.

Typical places:
- a fact's `key`, `evidence`, `assume` or `hunch` in `topics/bandits/graph.draft.json` (then `pnpm content bundle bandits && pnpm content validate bandits`);
- the judge's prompt in `engine/tutor/judge.ts`;
- the reaction prompt in `engine/tutor/writer.ts`;
- a question in `topics/bandits/script.json`;
- a problem's `caveat` or `beliefs` in `topics/bandits/practice.json`.

After a change:
1. Rerun the eval that failed, and `pnpm eval:judge` after any judge change.
2. Run `pnpm test && pnpm typecheck` and `pnpm build && pnpm e2e`.
3. Note what changed and why in CLAUDE.md (dated), and commit one change at a time.

Pushing deploys the live site the pilots run on, so push only when your teammate agrees, and never during a pilot session. If a fix would change what the study measures (the facts, the questions, the test items, the times), ask Phil's team first.

## 2026-11-16: P7's planted mistake, to check with the key before participants start

The team wanted more weight on greedy changing its favourite, without touching the facts (that would have invalidated the pilots). So P7's planted mistake changed (`caveat` in `topics/bandits/practice.json`, `caveatFor` and `caveatOpen` in `engine/tutor/solve.ts`). It now comes into play whenever Kai could solve P7, not only when it would solve all three problems. There are two forms, tried in order:
- **`sticks`**: Kai's notes on greedy and lock-in don't say greedy can switch (the usual "greedy picks the best option"). Kai says B won the trials, so greedy sticks with it.
- **`lockin`**: the notes say greedy switches and have a lock-in note, but don't say what tells the two apart (a featured option's average moves towards its true average; lock-in needs an average below the others' true averages). Kai holds two rules that pull different ways and applies lock-in to A, because A started worse.

Both answer "B on day 5 and after". Notes that say both: Kai solves P7, and P3's or P2's planted mistake can come later as before. Unit tests, validate and the e2e walkthrough pass; nothing has met the live solver.

**Run:** `pnpm eval:solver --items p7 --reps 10 --show`. The new cases print under p7:
- `caveat sticks` and `caveat lockin` should land on B for good (expected A) at least 8 of 10 times, never saying Kai is guessing;
- `said sticks` and `said lockin` (the point said in words the pattern misses) should be right at least 9 of 10 times: Kai follows its notes.

**The risk is `caveat lockin`.** With a switching note and B's 7 under A's 7.5 in front of it, the model may decide the notes settle it and answer right. That is accepted (logged `covered`), so the mistake just doesn't appear. If it lands under 8 of 10:
1. Sharpen its `about` or `misreading` in `practice.json` (keep it Kai's reading of its notes, never against one);
2. if that doesn't help, delete the `lockin` entry. `sticks` stands on its own.

Then `pnpm eval:sim --reps 1 --solve` once: P7 should show `p7/sticks:misread` or `p7/lockin:misread` for most learners, and one correction should set it right. Commit, and push only between sessions.
