Why V16 and V18 went

Both reduce to one step that form A already asks twice: within one round, the bonuses differ only through the tries, so the same tries mean the same bonus, and the higher average wins.

  • V16 (A 8.0 from 100 tries, B 7.9 from 2, C 3.0 from 100; which could greedy and UCB pick?) is T4 in another setting. C has A's tries and a lower average, exactly T4's D against B; B against A depends on the bonus sizes, exactly T4's A against B. Its greedy half (greedy picks only A) is the first step of T1 and T12.
  • V18 (round 9 after one try each: greedy and UCB pick the same) is the same step with every option at one try. T4 (B against D) and T16 (the bonuses in round 200 differ only through the tries) already ask it.

What form A never asks is how big the bonus is (the setting c, the team's F14) and what exploring costs. The two new items ask exactly those, so form B now covers ground form A doesn't, while still needing the same rules.

V19: The price of exploring replaces V16

Comparison, far transfer. Facts: F4 and F5 (greedy follows the leader, never re-tries what looks worse), F9 and F11 (UCB picks the highest score; an unpicked bonus keeps growing), plus the lesson's "exploring too much wastes rounds on options already known to be worse". Estimate 40%.
Two apps recommend one of three podcasts: one uses greedy, the other UCB with c chosen to fit ratings from 0 to 10. Every listen is rated, and each rating lands within 2 points of the podcast's true average, which the apps don't know: A 7, B 6 and C 3. Both apps start by trying each podcast once and happen to get the same trial ratings: A 9, B 4 and C 1. Over the next 1,000 listens, which app can expect the higher total rating?
  1. aThe greedy app correct
  2. bThe UCB app learner: exploring always pays, so UCB always does better than greedy
  3. cAbout the same for both apps Kai's F11 belief (an unpicked bonus stays the same, so UCB soon stops trying B and C), or learner: exploring costs nothing once the best is found
  4. dIt depends on A's next few ratings learner: overlooks that A's average can't fall below 5, above B's 4, so greedy never leaves A
Reasoning
After the trial, greedy's favourite is A, which is truly the best. A's ratings never fall below 5, so its average stays above B's 4 and C's 1: greedy picks A every round. UCB keeps coming back to B and C now and then, because their bonuses keep growing while they aren't picked, and each of those listens is rated lower than A's would be. Exploring has a price when the first favourite happens to be right.
Rubric
2 points: greedy stays on A, which is truly the best (A's average can't fall below B's 4); UCB keeps spending listens on B and C (their bonuses keep growing), and those cost rating. 1 point for one of the two.
Key check
Follows without simulation: greedy picks the truly best podcast every round, so no rule can expect more, and UCB must leave A again and again since an unpicked bonus grows without limit. Simulated (200 runs each): greedy picked A on every listen; UCB with c = 5 spent 58 to 138 of 1,000 listens on B or C and collected 6,893 against greedy's 6,997 on average (behind in 189 of 200 runs; c = 10: behind in 200 of 200).
Why "c fits"
With a c far too small for 0-to-10 ratings (c = 1), UCB never left A within 1,000 listens and (c) would be right. The stem rules that out by saying c fits the ratings.

V20: Ratings out of 100 replaces V18

Comparison, far transfer. The team's T10 (the old anchor, stashed when ε-greedy left the lesson) with greedy in ε-greedy's place. Facts: F14 (c sets the bonus's size; the bonus doesn't adapt to the rewards observed), F9, F4. The only item in either form on c. Estimate 30%.
Two music apps rate songs between 0 and 1: one picks songs with greedy, the other with UCB, with c chosen to fit ratings between 0 and 1. A developer switches both apps to ratings between 0 and 100 (every rating is multiplied by 100) and changes nothing else, so c stays as it was. Whose behaviour changes?
  1. aNeither app's Kai's F14 belief (the bonus adapts to the observed rewards)
  2. bBoth apps' learner: any change to the ratings changes both rules
  3. cOnly the UCB app's: it explores much more learner: larger numbers mean larger bonuses
  4. dOnly the UCB app's: it explores much less correct
Reasoning
Greedy's picks stay the same: multiplying every rating by 100 keeps the order of the averages. UCB's averages are now 100 times larger, but its bonus is still set by c, not by the ratings. The bonus becomes tiny next to the gaps between averages, and UCB behaves almost like greedy.
Rubric
2 points: the bonus is set by c and doesn't grow with the ratings, so it becomes tiny next to the averages; greedy only compares averages, whose order doesn't change. 1 point for one of the two.
Key check
Simulated again for the greedy version (four songs, true averages 0.7, 0.6, 0.5, 0.3, 2,000 rounds, 200 runs): with c = 0.5 the UCB app ended mostly on a worse song in 0 runs at the original scale and in 42 after multiplying by 100 (c = 1.41: 0 and 38). It spent 8.6% of rounds away from its own favourite before and 0.5% after, about greedy's 0.4%. Greedy's picks are identical at both scales.
Changes
"App 1 / app 2" became "the greedy app / the UCB app", so learners don't have to keep track of which number is which rule. The options are otherwise T10's.

Multiple-choice checks applied

  • Every wrong option is a named misconception (a Kai belief for a fact, or a known learner one), and none can be ruled out without the rules: no out-of-range or joke options.
  • Options state predictions only, are parallel, and the key is never the longest option (V19's key is the shortest; V20's two "Only the UCB app's" options are the same length).
  • No vague degrees: V19 asks which app, V20 which app and in which direction. No two options are true under one belief.
  • Settings of their own (podcasts, music ratings), never the lesson's or the practice's. Every number in V19's stem is used: the true averages say A is best, the ±2 band and the trial ratings show A's average can't fall below B's.
  • No calculation needed: both are answered from the rules; V19 needs one comparison (7 − 2 = 5 is above 4).
