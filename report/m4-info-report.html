Docendo study, EPFL CS-411 Digital Education

Participant information

Thank you for considering taking part. We are a student team in EPFL's CS-411 Digital Education, studying how people learn by explaining a topic to an AI classmate. Please read this page before you start.

Where
docendo-jade.vercel.appwith the personal code we send you
When
9 to 15 November 2026start any time in this week
How long
About an hourplus 5 minutes, not timed
Can you take part? Yes, if all of these hold:
  • you are 18 or older and study computer science or another engineering field at EPFL or RWTH;
  • you have passed a course on probability or statistics;
  • you have not studied multi-armed bandits (or algorithms for them, such as UCB), and have no formal training in reinforcement learning, such as a course. A rough idea of what reinforcement learning is, is fine.
If one does not hold, please tell us before you start: your answers could not be used.

What you will do

1 Short quiz8 minA few questions about your background, and some maths and statistics.
2 Read a lesson12 minA short text on multi-armed bandits, in three chapters.
3 Teach Kai16 minExplain the lesson to Kai, an AI classmate who missed it, by typing or speaking.
4 Practice12 minThree practice problems.
5 Final test12 minFive multiple choice questions, each with a short reason in your own words.
6 Your view≈ 5 minA few questions about how it went, then your score and the worked solutions.
Please plan an uninterrupted hour somewhere you can concentrate. Your code works once, and every step has a time limit and moves on by itself, so a long break would cut a step short. Use only the website: no search engines, chatbots, notes or copying. A dictionary for English words is fine. Please don't talk about the study with anyone who might take part, until 15 November.

Your rights

  • Taking part is voluntary and unpaid. You can stop at any time, without giving a reason.
  • There are no known risks.
  • You can have your data deleted until 25 November 2026: send us your access code. After that, results are shared with the course staff in anonymous form, and may be used in a course report or a scientific paper.

What we record

  • Your answers in the quiz, the practice and the test, your messages to Kai, and your answers at the end.
  • How you use the site: pages, clicks, time per step, switching tabs, typing and pasting, attempts to copy the lesson.
  • If you speak to Kai, only the written transcript is kept, not the audio.

Everything is stored under a random session number, never your name, email, IP address or access code. Your messages to Kai are processed by AI models through OpenRouter, only by providers that do not store them.
