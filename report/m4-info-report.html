Docendo study, EPFL CS-411 Digital Education

Participant information

Thank you for considering taking part. We are a student team in EPFL's course CS-411 Digital Education: Andrii Negrub, Phil Gustke and Rafael Cruz. We study how people learn by explaining a topic to an AI classmate. Please read this page before you start; it takes three minutes.

Can you take part? Yes, if all of these hold:
  • you are 18 or older and study computer science or another engineering field at EPFL or RWTH;
  • you have passed a course on probability or statistics;
  • you have not taken a course on multi-armed bandits or reinforcement learning, or learned about them another way.
If one does not hold, please tell us before you start: your answers could not be used.

What you will do

You take part online at docendo-jade.vercel.app, with a personal access code we send you. The code works once. You can start at any time during [dates of the three-day window]. The session takes about an hour, plus about five minutes of questions at the end that are not timed.

1 Short quiz8 minA few questions about your background and some maths and statistics.
2 Read a lesson12 minA short text on multi-armed bandits, in three chapters.
3 Teach Kai16 minExplain the lesson to Kai, an AI classmate who missed it, by typing or speaking.
4 Practice12 minThree practice problems.
5 Final test12 minFive multiple choice questions, each with a short reason in your own words.
6 Your view≈ 5 minA few questions about how it went, then your score and the worked solutions.

Please plan an uninterrupted hour somewhere you can concentrate. Every step has a time limit and moves on by itself when the time is up, so a long break would cut a step short. Use only the website: no search engines, chatbots, notes or copying. A dictionary for English words is fine.

Your rights

  • Taking part is voluntary and unpaid. You can stop at any time, without giving a reason.
  • There are no known risks beyond an hour at a screen.
  • You can have your data deleted until 25 November 2026: send us your access code. After that, results are shared with the course staff in anonymous form, and may be used in a course report or a scientific paper.

What we record

  • Your answers in the quiz, the practice and the test, your messages to Kai, and your answers at the end.
  • How you use the site: pages, clicks, time per step and per chapter, switching tabs, typing and pasting, attempts to copy the lesson.
  • If you speak to Kai, only the written transcript is kept, not the audio.

Everything is stored under a random session number, never your name, email or IP address, and your access code is not stored with your answers. Your messages to Kai are processed by AI models through OpenRouter, only by providers that do not store them.

Questions?

Write in the study's WhatsApp group, or to any of us: Andrii Negrub [email], Phil Gustke [email], Rafael Cruz [email]. By starting the session and ticking the consent box on the first page, you confirm that you have read this page and agree to take part.

You help us understand how teaching others helps people learn, and how AI can support it. Thank you.
